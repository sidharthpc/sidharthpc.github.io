Professional Summary

Computational mechanics researcher with a PhD from the Indian Institute of Technology Madras (IITM) on phase-field modeling of fracture in heterogeneous and multiphysics systems. Developed exponential finite element formulations that capture curved crack paths accurately on coarse meshes, extended them to functionally graded and orthotropic materials, and released them as an Abaqus user element and an open-source MOOSE code. Author of 12 peer-reviewed publications (6 journal articles, 5 proceedings papers, 1 book chapter) and 9 international conference presentations, with a 2023 ASME PVP student paper prize and the 2025 IIT Madras Institute Research Award. Spent 20 months as a DAAD fellow at the Institute of Mechanics and Computational Mechanics, Leibniz University Hannover, jointly supervised by Prof. Fadi Aldakheel, building coupled mechanical, electrochemical and fluid-flow fracture models of PEM water electrolyzers validated against experimental data. Experienced in Abaqus and FEniCSx development, with university-level teaching and student mentoring in finite element analysis.

Research Experience

Research Engineer (DAAD Fellow) — Structural & Multiphysics Simulation 1 Oct 2024 – 9 Jun 2026
Institute of Mechanics and Computational Mechanics (IBNM), Leibniz University Hannover, Germany
  • Developed a fully coupled mechanical-electrochemical-fluid flow model of a multi-layer proton exchange membrane water electrolyzer cell, establishing how clamping pressure reshapes the internal pore structure and closing a key gap in the literature.
  • Quantified how clamping pressure trades off electrical performance against fluid flow within the cell, validating against experimental polarization data to identify 5–8% compression as the practical range for electrolyzer cell design.
  • Extended the coupled multiphysics model to a fully non-isothermal formulation by incorporating localized Joule heating in the catalyst layers, capturing evaporation and condensation effects to more accurately predict cell behaviour at high current densities.
  • Linked catalyst-layer fracture and degradation to mechanical compression and electrochemical activity, showing that compression improves performance early on but accelerates long-term damage, setting practical compression limits for cell lifetime.
Graduate Researcher (PhD) — Fracture & Damage Tolerance Modeling 7 Sep 2020 – 16 Jun 2026
Indian Institute of Technology Madras, Dept. of Structural Engineering, Chennai, India
  • Formulated an algorithm with custom exponential finite element shape functions that enables accurate simulation of complex, curved crack paths for the first time, leading to a 20–30% improvement in accuracy when coarse elements are used.
  • Extended the custom finite-element formulation from homogeneous media to functionally graded materials (FGMs) for phase-field fracture simulations, matching the accuracy of a mesh with 80% more elements while adding virtually no extra computation time.
  • Deployed the formulation as a custom Abaqus user-element subroutine for orthotropic, fiber-reinforced materials, predicting crack paths as accurately as advanced XFEM methods while cutting peak-load errors by 4.43% with just 2% added computation time.
Structural Research Assistant (M.Tech) Aug 2017 – Apr 2019
National Institute of Technology Karnataka, Surathkal, India
  • Analysed thermal buckling and vibration-mode behaviour of FGM plates using FE models and predicted peak loads accurately.
  • Validated buckling predictions against generalized shear-deformation theory solutions, and established model reliability.
